Services

What we build, from basement renovation to the concrete patio

Six kinds of work make up most of our calendar. Each one is quoted on its own line, built by our own crew and trusted subs, and inspected where the code says it has to be.

01

Basement renovation

Most basements we open up have bare foundation walls and a slab that stays cold into June. We fix the envelope first: DMX or Delta-FL dimpled membrane on the concrete, a stud wall held off the foundation, ROCKWOOL batts, then drywall. Second units and new bathrooms need a permit under the Ontario Building Code, and we pull it.

Typically included

  • Framing, ROCKWOOL insulation and vapour control
  • Dimpled membrane or insulated subfloor over the slab
  • Egress windows and fire separation for second suites
  • Electrical, pot lights and HVAC runs
  • Three-piece bathroom, wet bar or laundry
Learn more about basement renovation

02

Kitchen renovation

A kitchen schedule lives or dies on order of operations. Cabinets go in, then quartz or porcelain slab counters get templated, never the other way around. Soft-close hardware on every door and drawer is standard, not an upgrade.

Typically included

  • Demolition, disposal and floor protection
  • Layout changes with plumbing and electrical rough-in
  • Cabinet install and slab counters
  • Tile backsplash, under-cabinet lighting, paint
Learn more about kitchen renovation

03

Bathroom renovation

Bathrooms fail behind the tile. Every shower we build gets Schluter-KERDI on the walls and a KERDI tray or sloped mortar bed, with DITRA under the floor tile. We flood-test the pan for a day before any tile goes on.

Typically included

  • Gut to studs and new supply lines
  • Schluter-KERDI and DITRA waterproofing
  • Tub-to-shower conversions, glass enclosures
  • Heated floors and an exhaust fan vented outside
Learn more about bathroom renovation

04

Flooring installation

Hardwood, engineered, LVP and tile. We level the subfloor before anything goes down, because most squeaks and gaps start there. Then we tell you plainly which product suits the room.

05

Concrete patios and walkways

Ontario winters find every shortcut in a slab. We dig out, compact a granular base in lifts, and pour air-entrained concrete so it survives freeze-thaw. Control joints go where the cracks want to go.

Typically included

  • Excavation and grading away from the house
  • Compacted Granular A base
  • Broom, smooth or exposed-aggregate finish
  • Steps, landings and sealing
Learn more about concrete patios

06

Backyard and landscaping builds

Interlock, retaining walls, decks and fences. Deck footings go below the frost line, roughly 1.2 metres down around here, so the boards stay level after the first winter. A deck attached to the house needs a permit, and we draw it up for you.

Typically included

  • Interlock patios and walkways
  • Retaining walls with clear stone drainage
  • Pressure-treated or composite decks
  • Wood and aluminum fencing, sod and beds
Learn more about backyard and landscaping builds

And the rest of the house

Drywall and taping, trim and baseboard, doors, interior painting and additions. If it's a job you'd rather not coordinate yourself, send us the details and we'll price it the same way as everything else.

Process

How a job runs, from first visit to final walkthrough

Four stages. You always know which one you're in and who to call about it.

  1. 01

    Site visit and measure

    We measure every room we're touching and check the things that change a price: panel capacity, drain locations, moisture on the foundation, what's under the old floor.

    You get: a visit from the person who would actually run your job, not a salesperson.

  2. 02

    Written quote and design

    A line-item quote with a fixed price and HST on its own line. Layout drawings and finish selections get settled here, before a single trade is booked.

    You get: the fixed-price quote, drawings, a materials list and a start date.

  3. 03

    Build with a dedicated lead

    One project lead runs the job from demolition to final paint. They book trades, meet the inspector and send you a short update at the end of each day.

    You get: one phone number for the whole job and a swept, cleared site every evening.

  4. 04

    Walkthrough and warranty

    We walk every room with you and write down anything that isn't right. It gets fixed before the final payment is due.

    You get: a closed permit, product warranties and a [X]-year workmanship warranty in writing.

LVP, in most basements. It is waterproof, it floats over the slab and it shrugs off the humidity swings that come with being below grade. Solid hardwood should not go on a concrete slab. Engineered hardwood can work over a dry slab with a proper subfloor if you want real wood underfoot, but it costs more and is less forgiving if water ever gets in.

Poured concrete costs less up front and lasts for decades if it is air-entrained and placed on a properly compacted base. Interlock costs more, but single pavers can be lifted and relaid if the ground settles, and it does not crack the way a slab can. For a plain rectangle, concrete is good value. For curves, steps or soft ground, interlock is usually the better call.
